## Find the applicable route

Identify whether the current request is new discovery, continuation, search, audit, local repair, handoff, release, live operations, or a change to the workflow. For a continuing game, inspect its checkpoint, exact files and accepted sources, open the [Game Model workflow](references/game-model-workflow.md), [core contract](references/core-contract.md), and the applicable parts of the [game disciplines](references/v4/index.md). Use the project's existing source of truth and an explicit mapping if necessary. Source-to-intent, requirement ID, dependency, owner, task, artifact, evidence, reviewer, and next step must remain traceable. The historical approvals and requirement registries in `assets/approved-baseline-4.0.json`, `assets/requirements-4.0.json`, and `assets/user-sources-3.0.json` are scoped sources. Preserve accepted musts and distinguish unavailable proof from an inapplicable requirement.

Review the actual stage catalog in `assets/stage-catalog.json`, the product's maturity, and the host operation that can be performed. Read the available installed specialist before handing over. The catalog links studio, concepts, preproduction, assets and build, QA, release, and liveops. Route a local defect to the smallest competent owner, name that owner, and open only the affected requirements and checks. For example, delegate an accepted game's resume/input repair to Build and retain the signed-off loop, world and levels. Ask QA for targeted regressions and mark a specific physical-device rerun pending until its receipt exists. Audit and toolkit work do not imply new concepts or release.

- Studio owns the lawful identity, editable sources and verified setup. Concepts presents feasible and genuinely distinct candidates for the user's choice.
- Preproduction owns the coupled GDD/TDD, typed rules and catalogs, and design-derived oracles. Assets owns reproducible sources and media evidence. Build implements contractual behavior and integrates both.
- Independent QA supplies scoped verdicts. Release binds the exact build, store, policies, commerce, rollout, and recovery. LiveOps observes actual outcomes and routes defects and approved changes.
- Parallel assets and build only where files and obligations can be independently owned. Agree and pin their shared camera, input, and model interfaces before dependent work. The director integrates the outcome.

## Tie decisions to the game

Keep production effort, topology, progression, action fantasy, camera, input, creative direction and commerce fit as separate choices. One game may need compact worlds, evolving scenes, or a large world. Small games need no fixed-arena default. Include systems, characters, equipment, encounters, progression, learning, economy, and persistence where the chosen game actually needs them. Show how changed contexts alter the player's decisions, how rewards are usable, and how effects carry across systems. Appearance, count of levels, or more numbers do not establish depth.

Use the [production contract](references/v4/production.md) to verify each applicable capability, recipe, transformation, attempt, assembly, argument use, and due evidence. Keep the 45 operation classes and X01–X11 as a reference rather than presuming all apply. Preserve exact ownership, hashes, inputs, interfaces, paths, acceptance, costs, and stop in handoffs. Choose actual discipline owners from research, systems, world, progression, experience, creative, commerce, and lifecycle. Separate solver contact, damage policy, lifecycle, and durable grants. Immediate hit feedback follows simulation. Durable grants need their own receipt.
